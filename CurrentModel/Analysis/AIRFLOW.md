# Airflow analysis: impeller, ducts and air-multiplier thrusters

Generated by `airflow.py` from the current CAD dimensions. Rerun it after changing the model.

## Inputs

- Air: ρ = 1.204 kg/m³ (20 °C, sea level).
- **Ducts:** 8 × Ø24 mm, 139 mm long, with the mouth at r = 77 mm on the keel.
- **Stem bore:** Ø13 mm.
- **Air-multiplier slot:** at a radius of 22 mm, currently 0.8 mm wide.
- **Impeller:** Ø88 mm, eye (blade inlet) radius 22 mm, blade height 12→9 mm.
- **Coefficients (the main uncertainties):**
  - slot C_d = 0.85, C_v = 0.95
  - thrust augmentation φ = 1.3 (published Coanda ejectors: 1.1–1.6)
  - fan static pressure coefficient ψ = 0.3, fan static efficiency η = 0.35
  - motor efficiency 0.75
  - loss coefficients K: duct entry 0.5, duct bends 0.3, dead end turn 1.2, stem entry 0.5, stem dump 1.0

## 1. What would hovering take?

- **Estimated all-up mass:** 887 g (hull 390, printed propulsion 250, motor 50, servos 107, esc 25, electronics 35, wiring 30).
- **Hydrogen lift:** the 12.7 L above the main deck gives 14 g, so thrust has to carry about 873 g, i.e. 8.6 N.
- **Jet speed sets the airflow needed:** thrust is φ·ṁ·V_j and jet power is ½ṁV_j², so a slower jet needs less power but more airflow.

| Jet speed V_j (m/s) | Air flow needed (L/s) | Jet power (W) | Slot area per thruster (mm²) | Duct speed in Ø24 (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 365 | 49 | 3576 | 101 |
| 25 | 219 | 82 | 1287 | 60 |
| 40 | 137 | 132 | 503 | 38 |
| 60 | 91 | 198 | 223 | 25 |

**Conclusion: hovering on fan thrust isn't feasible at this scale.**
- **Slow jets:** below about 25 m/s the airflow runs to hundreds of L/s. That needs ducts of Ø60 mm or more and an impeller eye bigger than the whole shaft.
- **Fast jets:** above 40 m/s the jet power alone is over 100 W, before any losses. With fan and motor efficiency, the motor would need more than 400 W.
- **So the bench demo's job is to show controlled, vectored thrust, not to hover.**

Where the pressure goes (3S case):

| Item | Pa | Share |
|---|---|---|
| slot (useful: makes the jet) | 291 | 48 % |
| duct entry | 6 | 1 % |
| duct friction | 3 | 0 % |
| duct bends | 3 | 1 % |
| turn into stem | 158 | 26 % |
| stem friction | 12 | 2 % |
| stem dump into ring | 132 | 22 % |

**Findings:**
1. **Fan speed, not motor power, is the limit.** On an 88 mm open impeller, the A2212's loaded speed caps the plenum pressure (about 600 Pa on 3S, about 1,070 Pa on 4S) before the motor reaches its power.
2. **The 0.8 mm slot makes a small, fast jet** that spends its power on speed rather than on moving air.
3. **The Ø13 mm stem is a choke:** air in it moves about as fast as in the slot, so it throws away a similar amount of pressure.

## 4. Recommended design point

The best feasible combination in the sweep: **A2212 1400KV, 3S**.

| | Now (v0) | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| Air-multiplier slot | 0.8 mm | **1.6 mm** |
| Stem bore | Ø13 mm | **Ø22 mm** (stem hole in the hull grows to Ø25) |
| Duct bore | Ø24 mm | Ø24 mm along its length (duct speed 13 m/s: friction is negligible), **flared to about Ø32 mm over its last 25 mm** so the wider stem enters it cleanly |
| Impeller eye radius | 22 mm | **30 mm** (inlet speed ≤ 22 m/s) |
| Impeller exit width b2 | 9 mm | **13 mm** |
| Motor / battery | A2212 1000KV, 3S | **A2212 1400KV, 3S** |
| Fan speed | — | **11988 rpm** (tip speed 55 m/s); motor shaft power used 150 W |
| Airflow | — | 48 L/s total, 5.9 L/s per thruster |
| Plenum pressure | — | 1102 Pa |
| Jet speed | — | 32 m/s |
| **Total thrust** | — | **2.36 N ≈ 240 gf** (30 gf per thruster) |

**Uncertainty:** with φ between 1.1 and 1.6 and fan efficiency between 0.30 and 0.45, the thrust range is **183–318 gf**. Measure φ and the fan curve on the bench, then rerun.

## 5. Plenum (fan chamber) sizing

The plenum only has to carry 48 L/s from the impeller rim (r = 44 mm) out to the duct mouths (r ≈ 77 mm), keeping the radial speed under 8 m/s:

| Radius mm | Height needed mm |
|---|---|
| 44 | 22 |
| 55 | 17 |
| 65 | 15 |
| 77 | 12 |

- **Impeller height:** the recommended impeller is about 15 mm tall at the rim, so the chamber needs about that height over the impeller (r ≤ 44 mm). The current floor at z = −70 above a keel at about −101 gives 31 mm there. That's enough, so **the floor can't come down much over the impeller**.
- **Outer plenum:** beyond the duct mouths (r > 89 mm) the plenum is dead volume. Its ceiling can slope down to meet the keel just outside the duct mouths, at about 20 mm height near r = 77 mm and zero beyond r ≈ 92 mm.
  - The keel skin outside that radius then no longer has to be airtight and can get lightening windows.
  - The floor shrinks to a sloped ring with lightening windows beyond the plenum.
  - That's the weight saving to build into the model: roughly 5 g per slice.

## 6. What to measure on the bench

1. **Fan curve:** plenum pressure (MPXV7002DP) and motor power (INA226) against throttle, with the thrusters blanked off and then open.
2. **Thrust augmentation φ:**
   - thrust of one thruster on the scale, with the ring fitted and with a bare stem of the same flow
   - slot exit speed from a pitot tube, or from Q/A
3. **Losses:** pressure at the stem inlet against the plenum. This checks the duct and turn losses.
4. **Rerun:** put the measured coefficients into `airflow.py` and rerun it before cutting new parts.
