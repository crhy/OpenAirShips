How it works

Eight identical 45° slices plug together into a smooth ellipsoid hull. Inside, everything except the air path is a lattice of large oval cells with hollow diamond junctions, so each slice weighs about 40 g.

A fan at the bottom of the central shaft pulls air in over the rounded intake at the top. The shaft narrows smoothly to the impeller, and the hull's own housing wall forms the fan's shroud. The fan pushes the air into 8 ducts that run inside the skin up to the equator.

At each seam, a swivelling Dyson-style air-multiplier ring turns the duct's air into a vectored jet. An MG90S servo turns it ±90°. The thrusters sit exactly on the equator, so the 8 of them give lift, yaw, surge and sway without pitching the ship.

An ESP32 runs the fan and servos from a Wi-Fi control page. Expected thrust is about 410 gf; the bench tests will measure it.

At a glance

  • Hull: 8 × ≈ 40 g PLA slices, ≈ 320 g in total, 0.86 mm airtight walls. Each slice prints without supports except inside one duct arch.
  • Fan: a 2207 1750 KV motor on 4S drives an 88 mm PETG impeller in the shrouded housing. It comes out through a bayonet hatch in the keel.
  • Thrusters: 8 air-multiplier rings with 1.6 mm slots, each swivelling ±90° on an MG90S servo.
  • Control: an ESP32 and a PCA9685, with a Wi-Fi control page and a link-loss failsafe.
  • Status: v0 is a tethered bench model for measuring and controlling thrust. Hover and hydrogen ballonets come with larger models.
