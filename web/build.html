How it works

Eight 45° slices plug together into a smooth ellipsoid hull. Inside, everything except the air path is a lattice of large oval cells with hollow diamond junctions.

A fan at the bottom of a smooth Ø66 central intake pulls air in over the rounded top. The hull's own housing wall forms the fan's shroud. The fan pushes the air into ducts that run inside the skin to the equator.

At each thruster seam a swivelling Dyson-style air-multiplier ring turns the duct's air into a vectored jet, turned ±90° by a servo. The thrusters sit exactly on the equator, so they give lift, yaw, surge and sway without pitching the ship.

The 4-thruster design uses bigger ducts and rings (twice the flow each) and alternating left and right slices. It makes slightly more thrust than 8 thrusters, and it's lighter.

Will it float?

Not yet on hydrogen alone. The 4-thruster Kobra Max model in lightweight (foaming) PLA weighs about 624–743 g and holds 124 L of hydrogen. That lifts 139 g, 19–22% of its weight. With the fan running it's 67–185 g short of hovering.

The bench-size 4-thruster model in fully foamed lightweight PLA can hover on its fan. Floating on hydrogen alone needs a hull about 1.7–1.9 m across: lift grows with size³, printed mass only about size^1.3.

The full mass, hydrogen and thrust budget covers every version and material.
